# Geometry engine recommendation for OGC 2026 spatial block scheduling

## Verdict up front

**Primary engine: integer-lattice bitmask (raster) engine at subgrid factor S=4, with positive-area rasterization computed once per (block, orientation, layer) from the exact fractional-vertex polygons; optional shapely exact check only at commit time for near-fit recovery. Do not build an NFP engine.** The decisive structural fact: placements are restricted to integer (x,y), so the true decision space is already a discrete lattice of ≤ 180×30 = 5,400 positions per bay. The NFP's core advantage — exact continuous sliding to contact — buys nothing on a lattice; complete enumeration of the *entire* decision space by bit-parallel correlation is cheaper than a single NFP query loop, and the raster test can be made **sound** (never accepts an infeasible placement), which is what the −1-on-infeasibility scoring demands.

Everything below is grounded in the actual training data (measured): 100–300 blocks/instance, **3–12 vertices per layer polygon (avg 6.8)**, avg 2.04 layers (max 4), ≤8 orientations, bays 32–179 × 15–29, block layer-0 bboxes median 10.2×8.7, max 46.8×28.8; 4,800 (block,orient,layer) polygons max per instance.

## 1. NFP: what it is, how it's computed, and why it loses here

Three computation families (Bennell & Oliveira tutorial; Rocha 2019 survey): **orbiting/sliding** (Mahadevan 1984; robust extension Burke et al. 2007) — simulates one piece sliding around another; fails or needs special handling on narrow concavity entries, internal holes, perfect-fit/perfect-slide positions, and loses contact under floating-point noise. **Slope diagrams** (Cuninghame-Green; Ghosh; Bennell et al. 2001) — merge edge lists by angle; interacting concavities are the hard case. **Minkowski sum via convex decomposition** — decompose both pieces into convex parts, take pairwise convex sums (O(m+n) each), union the results; the *union/merge step* is the robustness bottleneck (slivers, degenerate perfect-fit contacts appear as zero-area artifacts that must be detected, not discarded — Rocha 2019 builds a whole vertex-graph outline-extraction procedure just to keep them). Our polygons are small (≤12 vertices, mostly mildly non-convex → 2–4 convex parts), so any single NFP is easy; the problem is *count*:

**Tractability estimate for full precompute.** NFPs are needed per (shape_i @ orient_a, shape_j @ orient_b, layer k): C(300,2) ≈ 44,850 pairs × up to 8×8 = 64 orientation combos × ~2 shared layer levels ≈ **5.7M NFPs**. With shapely-based convex-decomposition + union at a realistic 100–500 µs each: **10–50 CPU-minutes**, memory at ~0.5–1 KB per stored NFP: **3–6 GB**. Both blow the "few minutes, 16 GB, 4 cores" envelope. Lazy per-touched-pair caching (only same-bay co-present pairs) cuts this to maybe ~10⁵–10⁶ computations under heavy local search — still tens of seconds to minutes of pure geometry overhead, plus the degenerate-case robustness engineering, plus you still have to intersect NFPs with the integer lattice at the end. Meanwhile the raster engine answers the same query for *all 5,400 positions at once* in ~1.6 ms (measured, below). NFP is strictly dominated for this problem.

The one NFP-family object that *is* free: the inner-fit region of the bay is a rectangle (bay is an axis-aligned integer rectangle), handled implicitly by scan bounds.

## 2. Recommended engine: positive-area bitmask rasterization + bit/FFT correlation

**Representation.** Subgrid resolution 1/S with **S=4** (cells 0.25×0.25). For each (block, orientation, layer) polygon P (translated so bbox-min = local origin, matching the reference-point convention after computing the fixed fractional offset to the reference vertex): mask M(P) = { cell c : area(c ∩ P) > 0 }. Max mask ≈ 188×116 bits ≈ 2.7 KB bit-packed; 4,800 masks ≈ **6 MB worst case (uint8), <1 MB packed** — memory is a non-issue. Bay occupancy: one grid per (bay, layer) ≤ 720×120 = 86,400 cells = 10.8 KB packed; plus suffix-OR grids `occ_ge[k] = OR_{j≥k} occ[j]` for the crane test (crane check of layer k at entry/exit = M ∧ occ_ge[k], directly encoding the j ≥ k rule). Time handled event-wise (≤ 2n events), stamping/erasing masks incrementally — even storing full snapshots per event is ≤ ~16 MB packed per bay-layer timeline.

**Queries.**
- Single-placement test: bitwise AND over ≤ 348 uint64 words → sub-µs in numba/C; ~20 µs even in plain numpy slicing (measured).
- **Full-bay feasible-position map** (all integer (x,y) at once): 2D cross-correlation of occupancy with the mask, subsampled at stride S. Measured with `scipy.signal.fftconvolve` on a 720×120 grid vs a 240×196-subcell mask: **1.6 ms per full scan**, bit-exact agreement with the naive test (integer-valued convolution, threshold 0.5, float32 error ≪ 0.5; still confirm the finally chosen cell with one exact bit-AND — sound at zero cost). Naive numpy: 20 ms. Numba packed-bit with 16 precomputed word-phase shifts (offsets are multiples of S=4 within 64-bit words): ~×10–50 further headroom.
- Shapely comparison point (measured): STRtree + intersection-area per single candidate position = **42 µs** → ~184 ms to scan one bay. The raster engine is ~**100× faster per position** and scans exhaustively.
- Contact/hole-filling scoring is free on the grid: popcount of (dilated mask ∧ occupancy) = touching-perimeter reward; column height profiles for bottom-left; distance transforms for "snugness".

**Budget check for the whole solver:** greedy insertion, 300 blocks × 3 bays × ~30 candidate entry events × 1.6 ms ≈ 43 s single-core with the lazy scipy version — already fits; the numba bit-op version leaves 95%+ of the wall clock to the actual optimization (assignment master + 4-core per-bay slaves), which is where rank points come from.

**Preprocessing cost.** Naive shapely cell-by-cell rasterization measured 26 s for a 100-block instance (1,472 polys, 17 µs/cell of shapely call overhead) — too lazy an implementation. Two fixes, either sufficient: (a) classify interior/exterior cells first with vectorized `shapely.contains_xy` on cell corners (nanoseconds/point), run exact `area(intersection)` only on the ~O(perimeter) boundary cells → ~×8 fewer shapely calls, ~3 s for 300 blocks; (b) exact scanline: clip each polygon to each 1/S-height band (trapezoid decomposition), mark cells whose x-interval coverage in the band has positive area — pure numba, O(V·S·H) per poly, milliseconds total. Rasterize lazily per orientation actually explored if you want it even cheaper.

## 3. Exactness argument (the part that protects you from −1)

The checker (verified in `utils.py`): normalizes via `buffer(0)`, AABB prefilter with strict `<` (edge/corner AABB touch already passes), flags collision iff `intersection.area > 0`, containment iff `outside.area > 0` (touching the bay wall is feasible), crane per the same j ≥ k polygon test; x,y are rounded to int.

1. **Soundness (no false accepts).** If M(P₁+t₁) ∩ M(P₂+t₂) = ∅ then area((P₁+t₁) ∩ (P₂+t₂)) = 0. Proof: any positive-area overlap region puts positive area into some cell c, hence area(c∩(P₁+t₁)) > 0 and area(c∩(P₂+t₂)) > 0, so c is in both masks — contradiction. Since t is integer and cells are 1/S with S integer, a translated mask is the mask shifted by S·t cells exactly — **no re-rasterization, no alignment error, ever**. Therefore a raster-feasible schedule passes the checker's Stage-4/crane geometry *by construction*; the trivially-feasible fallback then guarantees a valid submission unconditionally.
2. **Containment is exact in both directions.** Marked cells never straddle the bay boundary (bay corners are integers, cells are 1/S-aligned), so: all marked cells inside bay ⇒ area(P \ bay) = 0 (accepted by checker); any marked cell outside ⇒ area(P \ bay) > 0 (rejected by checker). Zero conservatism here, including flush wall contact.
3. **Grazing contacts are not over-rejected.** "area > 0" marking means a polygon whose boundary runs *along* a cell edge does not mark the far cell: two blocks flush along any 1/S-aligned line (in particular all integer-coordinate axis-aligned contacts, the dominant tight-packing case for these shapes) are **accepted**, matching the checker's edge-touching-is-feasible rule.
4. **Incompleteness (only approximation in the engine).** False rejections occur only when two boundaries pass through the *same* cell with positive area each but zero mutual overlap — i.e., true clearance < cell diameter √2/S ≈ 0.354 at S=4 along non-grid-aligned (sloped, 4-decimal-vertex) boundaries. Quantified: any placement with ≥ 0.354 clearance is accepted; the lost placements are the sub-0.35-clearance diagonal snuggles.
5. **Recovery of tight fits (optional, commit-time only).** For high-value rejected candidates (e.g., the position that would kill a tardiness), verify with shapely using the checker's own predicate (`buffer(0)`, `intersection.area > 0`) on the *identical* coordinates — deterministic agreement with the evaluator since it is the same library and doubles; accept only exact `area == 0.0`. Keep such placements on a separate "shapely-verified" list; everything else stays pure-raster. Raising S to 8 (masks ×4 memory, still trivial; FFT scan ~×4) shrinks the gap to 0.177 if profiling shows tight diagonal fits matter.

## 4. Literature → what to reuse on top of this engine

- **Bennell & Oliveira, "The geometry of nesting problems: a tutorial" (EJOR 2008) and "A tutorial in irregular shape packing" (JORS 2009)**: raster/grid is a first-class representation whose two classical weaknesses — memory and resolution error — are both voided here (5,400-cell bays; integer decision lattice). Use their taxonomy in the technical report to justify the choice.
- **Burke, Hellier, Kendall, Whitwell (Ops. Res. 54(3), 2006) BLF**: resolution-based bottom-left-fill + hill-climbing/tabu over the *insertion sequence*; 25/26 best-known at the time, minutes of runtime. Direct template: our EDD-based greedy = their BLF; sequence perturbation (swap/insert of due-date-adjacent blocks) is the cheap first metaheuristic layer. Their BLF resolves overlap by vertical hops — on our grid this is "next free y in the column map", O(1) with column bitmasks.
- **Chehrazad, Roose, Wauters (2021), semi-discrete BLF (arXiv:2103.08739)**: discretizes pieces/strip into vertical segment lists, BLF in *milliseconds* on ESICUP instances including rotations; explicitly argues discretization in one dimension loses little vs NFP and avoids NFP's numerical problems (citing Wauters et al. 2016). Independent evidence that a discretized engine at our resolution is competitive with NFP pipelines even for pure nesting — and our problem is coarser than ESICUP nesting.
- **Egeblad, Nielsen, Odgaard (EJOR 2007) fast neighborhood search**: allow overlapping intermediate states; minimum-overlap axis translation of one piece in O(mn log mn), guided local search on overlap penalties (also Umetani et al. 2009, GLS for overlap minimization). Grid analog is even simpler: the FFT correlation map *is* the overlap-area (popcount) landscape over all translations at once — pick argmin instead of requiring zero. This is the right repair/relocation move when re-timing blocks for tardiness: relocate the violator to min-overlap position, penalize, iterate. Reserve for the intensification phase.
- **Rocha (arXiv:1903.11139), robust NFP generation** + **Burke et al. 2007 complete-robust orbital NFP**: read as the catalogue of NFP failure modes (holes, interlocking concavities, perfect fits, precision loss) — i.e., the engineering bill you avoid by going raster.
- **Shapely 2.x engineering** (only needed at commit-verify and offline validation): vectorized ufuncs over geometry arrays, `STRtree.query(geoms, predicate=...)` with internal prepared geometries, `shapely.prepare()`; `contains_xy`/`intersection` array forms for fast rasterization. The provided checker already does AABB-prefilter + shapely; run it offline as the final gate before writing the solution.

## 5. Concrete build list

1. Rasterizer (numba scanline or contains_xy+boundary-exact), S=4, per (block, orient, layer), bit-packed uint64 rows; store S·t shift phases or use FFT maps.
2. Per-(bay,layer) occupancy + suffix-OR crane grids, event-timeline incremental stamp/erase; per-block stamped-ID grid (int16) for "who blocks me" queries feeding the overhang-precedence digraph.
3. Feasible-map kernel: fftconvolve (occupancy, mask) + stride-S subsample + exact AND confirm; batched over the ≤4 layers and crane grids in one pass (sum the layer-wise correlations; feasible iff all zero).
4. Scoring layers on the same maps: bottom-left tiebreak, contact popcount, tardiness/Z2/Z3 from the master assignment.
5. Commit-time shapely verifier mirroring `utils.py` predicates; final full-checker run before emitting operations.
