PER-PAPER SUMMARIES (most relevant first)

1. **He, Hong & Kim (2024), "Quick and situ-aware spatiotemporal scheduling for shipbuilding manufacturing," J. of Scheduling** — [link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10951-024-00804-1). THE closest ancestor of the OGC problem: KSOE (parent of the competition sponsor's client) data, 637–810 blocks, ≤10 bays, 6-month horizon. Two-phase decomposition: Phase I = MIP over *areas only* (bay assignment + start date, minimizing outsourced blocks + max monthly utilization imbalance); Phase II = per-bay independent IP for coordinates + rotation (0/90/270°); placement failures bounce back as "outsourced." 2h serial / 30min parallel vs 2 weeks manual. Validates exactly our two-level decomposition (assignment master + independent per-bay packing slaves) — but has NO layers, NO crane-lift constraint, and rectangle-level geometry.

2. **Lee, Lee & Choi (1996), "A spatial scheduling system and its application to shipbuilding: DAS-CURVE," Expert Syst. Appl.** (+ Lee & Lee 1995 AI Magazine "DAS: Intelligent Scheduling Systems for Shipbuilding") — [DAS-CURVE](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0957417496000103), [AI Magazine](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1609/aimag.v16i4.1173), [DAS project report](https://bmer.net/wp-content/uploads/2010/11/dasejor.pdf). The foundational work (KAIST + Daewoo, 1991–93): defined "spatial scheduling" = scheduling jobs each requiring physical space; polygonal layout on rectangular plates extended with a time axis; due dates imposed by erection schedule. Expert-system + layout heuristics, greedy day-by-day. Everyone since cites this as the origin.

3. **Koh, Park, Choi & Joo (1999/2011 line), two-phase block assembly scheduling; Largest Contact Area placement** — summarized in [Shang 2017 refs](https://www.hindawi.com/journals/mpe/2017/1923646/). Introduced the schedule-then-layout phase split and the LCA rule: among feasible positions pick the one maximizing contact perimeter with already-placed blocks/walls (compaction proxy). Cheap, effective scoring for irregular placement — directly reusable in our candidate scoring.

4. **Zheng, Jiang, Chen & Liu (2011), "Spatial scheduling algorithm minimising makespan at block assembly shop," IJPR 49(8)** — [link](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00207541003709536). Full math description of polygonal block intersection + a multistep greedy that switches placement strategy per decision step (corner-first, edge-alignment, etc.). Reported better than CPLEX and GA at large scale, and better than grid algorithm + manual scheduling on makespan, plate utilization, runtime, on-time delivery. Lesson: adaptive/staged greedy beats monolithic metaheuristics at production scale.

5. **Zhang & Chen (2012), "Solving the spatial scheduling problem: a two-stage approach," IJPR 50(10)** — [link](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00207543.2011.588623); companion "Block spatial scheduling modelling and application," IJPR 50(10) — [link](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00207543.2011.588629). Stage 1 = *agglomeration algorithm*: cluster blocks close in time+space+due date into "virtual blocks" to shrink instance; Stage 2 = NMIP on the reduced problem. Transferable idea: co-schedule sibling blocks (same due window, similar dwell) as composite shapes to reduce combinatorics.

6. **Kwon & Lee (2015), "Spatial scheduling for large assembly blocks in shipbuilding," CAIE 89:203–212** — [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360835215002296). Two-stage heuristic: dispatching priority rules (EDD/area/slack composites) for order, then *diagonal-fill* allocation = bottom-left-fill modified to grow simultaneously from bottom-left AND top-right corners, keeping the free space in one contiguous central band. Objectives: makespan + load balance; handles rotation, symmetric blocks, designated-workplace constraints. Diagonal fill is a drop-in upgrade of the baseline's pure bottom-left.

7. **Shang, Gu, Ding & Duodu (2017), "Spatial Scheduling Optimization Algorithm for Block Assembly," Math. Probl. Eng.** — [link](https://www.hindawi.com/journals/mpe/2017/1923646/). Models BSS as *time-constrained 3D bin packing* (time = 3rd axis). Best Contact Algorithm (BCA): position maximizing contact with existing blocks *including the time dimension* (i.e., contact with blocks that stay co-present), GA over block start times / sequence. Confirms GA-over-sequence + constructive-decoder is the standard metaheuristic architecture here.

8. **Ge & Wang (2018), "Spatial scheduling strategy for irregular curved blocks... MGACA," IJPR 56(9):3099–3115** — [link](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00207543.2017.1402135); and **Ge & Wang (2021), "Spatial scheduling for irregularly shaped blocks," CAIE** — [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360835220306550). Decision variables identical to ours minus layers: entry time, entry sequence, position, rotation angle of irregular polygons. 2021 paper: MIP maximizing plate utilization + minimizing total delay; placement via *standard-angle filling* — polygon simplified so all edges are multiples of 15°, reducing waste vs AABB simplification. Genetic-ant-colony hybrid over sequence. Message: literature approximates polygons; nobody does exact shapely-style geometry inside the search loop.

9. **Zhang, Li, Demeulemeester & Zhang (2024), "A 3D spatial resource-constrained project scheduling problem: model and heuristic," EJOR 319(3):943–966** — [link](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ejores/v319y2024i3p943-966.html). 4D time-space RCPSP; 36 priority rules (25 classic + 11 spatial variants) x 3 space-allocation strategies, with a *decision tree that picks the best rule combo per instance from instance features*. PSPLIB-derived instances; heuristic beats Gurobi beyond small sizes. The rule-portfolio + learned selector is a modern, report-friendly idea that fits our 4-core parallel budget (run portfolio, keep best).

10. **Frontiers Mar. Sci. (2026), "Scheduling optimization framework for irregular assembly blocks in cruise ship manufacturing" (ICBLSP)** — [link](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2026.1843859/full). Only paper found with an explicit *removal-obstruction* constraint: blocks must not obstruct exit paths of earlier-outbound blocks (flat transporters, horizontal ground paths, 4-side yard access). Placement scored by λ (obstruction of existing outbound blocks) + μ (hindrance to future inbound); outbound-before-inbound daily ordering (same as our EXIT-before-ENTRY!); GA with date-partitioned encoding. 237–362 blocks, 40 cells, 5–30 days. Closest in spirit to our crane constraint, but their obstruction is horizontal/transporter, not vertical layered lift.

11. **Ahn & Kim (2022), "Mathematical formulation and heuristic for spatial scheduling of mega-blocks," J. Ship Prod. Design 38(4):193–198** — [link](https://onepetro.org/JSPD/article-abstract/38/04/193/493575/); plus **shipyard spatial arrangement with free-space/unplaced-block handling, IJAMT 2018** — [link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00170-017-1525-1). Improved MILP for rectangle-based spatial scheduling; instances remain small for exact methods (tens of blocks). Confirms: exact methods top out far below 100 blocks; 300 blocks is firmly heuristic territory.

12. **Block Relocation Problem literature (container yards): Tanaka & Mizuno (COR 2018), Tanaka & Voß (EJOR 2022), Jin & Tanaka (EJOR 2022)** — [Tanaka BRP page](https://sites.google.com/site/shunjitanaka/brp), [restricted BRP IP](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221721002927), [LB survey arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.03347). Retrieval-priority stacking with LIFO blocking: exact B&B with strong lower bounds counting "blocking pairs." Not spatial-continuous, but the *blocking-pair lower bound* and precedence-digraph reasoning transfer directly to our crane-induced exit-precedence structure.

SYNTHESIS — THREE TECHNIQUES TO STEAL

1. **He/Hong/Kim's two-level decomposition, hardened**: assignment+timing master using areas/workload only (our Z2, Z3 are *pure assignment costs* — even cleaner than theirs), then per-bay independent placement slaves parallelized over 4 cores, with a *repair loop* where placement failures return to the master as constraints (their "outsourcing" = our re-assignment/delay). This is the literature-sanctioned architecture and matches our structural fact (3) exactly; we can cite it as the established baseline we extend.
2. **Contact/compaction placement scoring instead of pure bottom-left**: Koh's Largest-Contact-Area, Shang's time-aware Best-Contact (prefer touching blocks with similar exit dates so freed space is contiguous in time), and Kwon & Lee's diagonal fill (grow from two opposite corners; keeps a mid-bay corridor — which for us also reduces crane-exit blocking). Combined: score candidate positions by contact length weighted by co-presence duration + corner/edge preference; keep candidate generation on NFP/AABB-offset points.
3. **Rule-portfolio + adaptive selection (Zhang et al. EJOR 2024, Zheng 2011)**: run a portfolio of (sequencing rule x placement rule) constructive decoders across the 4 cores under the wall clock, seed the best into local search (GA/SA over sequence with greedy decoder is the literature standard); optionally select portfolio weights from instance features (R-spread, congestion ratio = Σ area·P / (bay area · horizon)). Robust against hidden-instance variety, cheap to defend in the report.

Also worth stealing at smaller scale: Zhang & Chen's agglomeration (pair small same-window blocks into composite shapes), and BRP-style blocking-pair counting as a fast surrogate/lower bound for crane feasibility during search.

WHAT THE LITERATURE DOES NOT COVER (novelty angles for the technical report)

- **The layered-polygon + vertical crane-lift (j≥k) constraint is absent from every paper found.** The only removal-obstruction work (Frontiers 2026 ICBLSP) is horizontal transporter paths on a grid. Nobody models per-layer collision with overhang allowed, and nobody exploits the induced *exit-precedence digraph* (tall block overhanging a short one ⇒ LIFO-like constraint). Formalizing this digraph, proving its acyclicity/maintenance rules, and using BRP-style blocking-pair bounds inside a spatial scheduler is a genuinely publishable novelty.
- **Exact polygon geometry in the loop**: literature simplifies (AABBs, 15°-angle fills, grids). Exact shapely/NFP-based candidate generation with integer positions + layerwise checks is beyond the published state of practice.
- **Rank-based multi-instance robustness** (never-infeasible fallback + anytime portfolio) has no analog in this literature — all papers optimize a single yard instance offline.
- **Scale check**: 300 blocks / 2–5 bays is at or above the largest published heuristic studies (Zheng ~real yard scale, He et al. 637–810 blocks but rectangles-only and 2-hour budget, Frontiers 237–362 grid cells); with exact polygons + layers + crane constraints under minutes of wall clock, we are strictly outside published territory — exact methods in this field die at tens of blocks, so a Gurobi/CP master over assignment (not geometry) is the right use of the solvers.
